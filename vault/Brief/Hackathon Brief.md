Sources: https://highschoolhackathon.org (with its judges and FAQ pages), the official Hacker Guide (PDF) and the Workshop 1 and 2 slides, read 12–13 Nov 2026. If the starter kit email says something different, the email wins. Update this note.

## What it is
A free, online, 48-hour AI hackathon for high school students, run by Veritas AI. Teams of 1 to 3, everyone enrolled in high school as of 14 Nov. You build a working AI project: a model that predicts, classifies, generates or recommends something, applied to a real problem in the track you choose.

## Tracks
Four tracks: **AI + Health, AI + Finance, AI + Climate, AI + Languages.** The exact challenge brief for each is only revealed at kickoff. Track fit is scored, so the brief comes first. We choose a track on 14 Nov.

## What we submit
One person submits for the whole team.
1. **Code.** A Google Colab notebook or a GitHub repo, viewable by anyone with the link. Test it in a logged-out browser.
2. **A short write-up** in the official template. The structure is in Write-up.
3. **A demo video, 2:00 at most.** Unlisted YouTube or a Drive link. The recommended shape is in Demo Script.
4. **The names of all team members**, and a citation for every dataset, model and code source we used.

## Ground rules
- All project code is written during the 48 hours. Before kickoff, learning, practising, forming the team, and browsing and loading datasets are fine.
- AI coding assistants and chatbots are allowed "the same way working engineers do". We must understand and be able to explain every part, and the write-up lists the AI tools we used. Finalists are asked about their choices live.
- Public datasets, pre-trained models, open-source libraries and tutorial code are allowed if cited. Submitting someone else's project means disqualification.
- Public or synthetic data only. No personal information without permission.
- Check how well it works across groups, and say so if it's uneven.
- Health and finance projects must state that they are prototypes, not medical or financial advice.
- Report the actual results, including disappointing ones.
- Everyone listed on the team must have contributed.
- Late submissions are not accepted, with no exceptions for technical problems in the final minutes. The organisers recommend submitting at least an hour early.

## Judging
- **Round 1:** every submission is reviewed blind by the Veritas AI team against the rubric. Reviewers don't see names or schools. The top projects advance.
- **Finals:** a 5-minute live demo plus questions from a new panel of AI researchers and engineers.
- Final ranking = 70% finals score + 30% Round 1 score.
