## What it is
A free, online, 48-hour AI hackathon for high school students, run by Veritas AI. Teams of 1 to 3. One working AI project, in one of four tracks.

**There is no kickoff call** and nothing to attend live this weekend. Everything is in the Project Briefs and the Hacker Guide.

**The organisers give no technical help** during the weekend: no debugging, no fixing errors, no choosing a model. They only help with problems submitting (contact@veritasai.com). We rely on each other, the tool documentation and our AI assistants.

## The rules (from the Project Briefs)
1. **Teams:** 1 to 3 high school students. Everyone listed must have contributed.
2. **Build during the weekend.** All project work between start and deadline. Learning tools, running starter notebooks and browsing datasets before is fine. Extending an older project is not.
3. **AI must be central.** AI or machine learning does the project's main job. A normal website or app built with AI coding tools does not count on its own.
4. **What we can use:** public datasets, pretrained models, AI APIs (for example the Gemini free tier), open-source libraries, tutorial code, coding assistants and chatbots. All of them are cited in the write-up. The team understands every part and can explain it to the judges.
5. **What we can't do:** submit someone else's project (disqualification), collect personal information without permission. Public or synthetic data only.
6. **One project, one track.** Chosen on the submission form. Resubmitting before the deadline is allowed as often as we like: judges see the latest version.
7. **Disclaimers:** a health or finance project says in the write-up that it is a prototype and not medical or financial advice. The exact lines are in [[Track Briefs]].
8. **Honest results,** including weak ones.

## What every project must show
The write-up and demo answer four questions:
1. **Who is it for?** One specific user and the moment they would reach for our tool.
2. **What goes in, what comes out?** A real input producing a result.
3. **Does it beat a simple baseline?** Baseline score next to model score.
4. **Where does it fail?** At least one example it gets wrong, and why we think so.

All results come from test data the model did not see in training.

**Make it your own.** Starting from a suggested project gets a complete but low-scoring submission. Adding at least one thing of our own scores higher: our own user or setting, our own data, or our own question. We say what we added in the write-up and on the form.

The organisers' quick test for an idea: *"Our project helps [a specific person] by taking [an input] and giving back [an output]."* If no usable data loads within about 30 minutes, switch dataset or idea.

## What we submit
One person submits for the whole team, on the submission form (link in the WhatsApp group). Complete means **working links to both code and write-up**.

| Item | Required | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Code | Yes | GitHub repo (we don't use Colab), viewable by anyone with the link. **Notebook saved with its outputs visible** |
| Write-up | Yes | About one page in the official template (see Write-up). Google Doc ("anyone with the link can view"), the GitHub README, or a PDF or Word upload |
| Demo video | **Optional, recommended** | Up to 2 minutes. Unlisted YouTube or Drive with "anyone with the link" |
| Team details | Yes | Track, and every member's name, email and school |
| Credits | Yes | Every dataset, pretrained model, tutorial and AI tool, with links |

The form also asks: project title, team or solo, team name, **starting point** (which suggested project, or our own idea), a **one-sentence pitch**, **what we added**, datasets/models/AI tools used, and whether we are new to AI.

Before submitting: open every link in a private window, check every name and email is spelled right, and **submit a first version a few hours early**, then resubmit as it improves. If the upload fails at the deadline, email contact@veritasai.com at once with the links.

## Judging
- **Round 1:** the Veritas AI team reviews every complete submission **blind** (no names or schools).
- **Finals:** 5 minutes live plus questions from a panel of AI researchers, at the award ceremony.
- Four criteria: **technical quality, impact, creativity, clarity.** Details and our plan: [[Rubric]]
